Self-Distilling Future Video Representations for World Action Models

Huiwon Jang1,2*, Suhyeok Jang1,2*, Sihyun Yu2, Hyeonmo Kang2, Seungku Kim2, Jinwoo Shin1,2
1RLWRLD, 2KAIST
*Equal Contribution
Paper arXiv Code

TL;DR: We introduce FRED, a framework that trains World Action Models to generate better future video representations from a single denoising step, by self-distilling from a momentum teacher that processes noisy ground-truth futures and by conditioning on observation history.

Abstract

World Action Models (WAMs) have emerged as a promising approach to learning generalist robot policies using rich spatiotemporal priors from pre-trained video diffusion models. A recent line of WAMs improves inference efficiency by conditioning a separate action decoder on intermediate future video representations extracted from a single denoising step, avoiding costly iterative denoising required for video generation. This design makes action prediction depend directly on the quality of these future representations. In this paper, we introduce Future REpresentation learning via self-Distillation with temporal context (FRED), a framework that trains WAMs to improve the quality of future video representations from noise in a single denoising step. Specifically, FRED uses self-distillation to predict future representations from noise, using representations extracted from ground-truth future observations as targets. We further condition the video model on historical observations, providing temporal context for predicting future representations better. Across diverse real-world manipulation scenarios, FRED consistently outperforms competing WAMs. It achieves particularly large gains on long-horizon and out-of-domain tasks under zero-shot environment generalization, and substantially improves human-to-robot skill transfer, reaching 50.0% task success on tasks absent from robot training data and demonstrated only in human videos, compared with 19.4% for the best competing WAM.

Method overview

FRED is built on the decoupled WAM architecture: a video diffusion model $\mathbf{v}_\theta$, whose first layers $\mathbf{f}_\theta$ produce an intermediate representation $\mathbf{h}$, and a diffusion-based action decoder $\pi_\psi$ conditioned on $\mathbf{h}$ and the proprioceptive state. Given the current observation $\mathbf{o}_t$ and a language instruction $\mathbf{c}$, the video model runs a single denoising step from Gaussian noise $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}$ to produce $\mathbf{h}$, and the action decoder predicts actions from $\mathbf{h}$ without generating a video. FRED adds two components to this architecture.

Self-distillation from noisy futures. The student $\mathbf{f}_\theta$ generates $\mathbf{h}$ from noise, while a momentum teacher $\mathbf{f}_\phi$ processes a lightly corrupted ground-truth future observation $\mathbf{o}^\tau_{t+1} = (1-\tau)\,\mathbf{o}_{t+1} + \tau\,\boldsymbol{\epsilon}$ under the same conditioning to produce the target $\mathbf{h}^\tau$:

$\mathbf{h} = \mathbf{f}_\theta(\boldsymbol{\epsilon} \mid \mathbf{o}_{t-H:t}, \mathbf{c}) \quad \text{and} \quad \mathbf{h}^\tau = \mathbf{f}_\phi(\mathbf{o}^\tau_{t+1} \mid \mathbf{o}_{t-H:t}, \mathbf{c})$

Patch-wise projection heads $\mathbf{g}_\theta, \mathbf{g}_\phi$ map both representations to distributions; the teacher logits are centered by their running mean $\mathbf{m}$ and sharpened with a lower temperature to prevent collapse. The self-distillation loss is a per-patch KL divergence:

$\mathbf{p} = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\mathbf{g}_\theta(\mathbf{h}) / T_1\right), \quad \mathbf{p}^\tau = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left((\mathbf{g}_\phi(\mathbf{h}^\tau) - \mathbf{m}) / T_2\right), \quad \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SD}}(\theta) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathrm{KL}\!\left(\mathbf{p}^\tau_i \,\|\, \mathbf{p}_i\right)$

History as temporal context. Both student and teacher are conditioned on a short history $\mathbf{o}_{t-H:t}$ rather than only the current frame, which provides motion cues (e.g., where the arm is heading) that disambiguate the future.

Training objective. The video model is trained with the video flow-matching loss plus self-distillation, and the action decoder with its own flow-matching loss. The action loss does not update the video model; the teacher is the EMA of the student, $\phi \leftarrow m\phi + (1-m)\theta$.

$\mathcal{L}_{\text{video}}(\theta) = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}(\theta) + \lambda_{\mathrm{SD}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SD}}(\theta) \quad \text{and} \quad \mathcal{L}_{\text{action}}(\psi) = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}(\psi)$

Analysis

A single denoising step is a future representation generator

Using DiT4DiT as the base WAM on RoboCasa Kitchen, task success is only weakly correlated with (a) one-step video generation quality ($r=0.24$) and (b) the quality of representations extracted from observed future videos ($r=0.49$), but strongly correlated with (c) the quality of future representations generated from noise in one step ($r=0.89$).

Recent WAMs achieve competitive performance with a separate action decoder that generates actions conditioned on intermediate hidden states of the video diffusion model, obtained from a single forward pass with Gaussian noise $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}$ as input. This design avoids iterative denoising with large video backbones, but it also means that policy performance depends on how accurately these hidden states capture future robot-object dynamics. We therefore interpret the hidden states as future representations and the video diffusion model as a future representation generator, $\mathbf{h} = \mathbf{f}_\theta(\boldsymbol{\epsilon} \mid \mathbf{o}_t, \mathbf{c})$. The generated representation captures the semantics of plausible futures, yet it may still lack details needed for control (e.g., target object location or end-effector pose), since the generator must infer them from the current observation alone.

Is the quality of this generated future representation what matters for control? In Figures (a) and (b), task success is only weakly correlated with one-step video generation quality ($r=0.24$) and representation quality on observed future videos ($r=0.49$). In contrast, in Figure (c), the quality of future representations generated from noise shows a stronger correlation with task success ($r=0.89$). This supports our view of the video diffusion model as a future representation generator and suggests that improving the representations directly used for action generation may be particularly relevant to control performance.
